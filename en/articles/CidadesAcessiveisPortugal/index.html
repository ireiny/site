Five affordable cities in Portugal

House prices, population, employers and what to buy in Vila Real, Guarda, Bragança, Portalegre and Castelo Branco.

Irina Abasheva · updated 18 November 2026

Outside Lisbon and Porto, the budget needed to buy a home changes considerably. An affordable property still needs to suit your lifestyle or have a clear market for rental. Here is what lies behind the lower prices in five cities in Portugal’s interior.

In Idealista’s October 2026 comparison, these were the five most affordable district and autonomous-region capitals. Smaller towns and villages outside the comparison may be cheaper.

How much does housing cost?

The median is the midpoint of the sample: half the values are lower and half are higher. It provides a city-level benchmark. A new apartment with parking can cost considerably more.

The population is growing, and age matters

Population increased in all five municipalities. Deaths outnumbered births, and net migration drove the growth. Bragança was the only one of the five to grow faster than the country as a whole.

5. Vila Real

A university city near the Douro

Vila Real is in northern Portugal’s interior.

Shopping. Nosso Shopping and Continente supermarket.
Transport. Intercity coaches to Porto and Lisbon leave from the city bus station. The A4 is a convenient route to Porto by car. The city has no passenger rail service.
Climate. Average temperatures are +6.3 °C in January and +21.8 °C in July. July’s average daily high is +28.8 °C (IPMA, 1991–2020).

Education: Universidade de Trás-os-Montes e Alto Douro — UTAD.
Healthcare: Hospital de São Pedro, ULS de Trás-os-Montes e Alto Douro.

Of the five municipalities, Vila Real is closest to the national ratio: 2.56 residents aged 15–64 per resident aged 65 or over, compared with 2.76 nationwide. The figures cover the entire municipality.

What investors could buy

A T1 or T2 near UTAD, the hospital or good transport links could suit students, lecturers, healthcare workers or working couples. A T3 may work for room-by-room rental if the layout suits it and local rents support the numbers. Factor in competition from student residences and possible summer vacancies.

What to consider

Vila Real is the most expensive city in the selection. A good location and winter comfort should justify the difference in price.

4. Guarda

Industry and logistics near Spain

Guarda is in central inland Portugal, near Spain and the Serra da Estrela.

Shopping. La Vie Guarda shopping centre and Continente Modelo supermarket.
Transport. Lisbon is accessible by Intercidades train or coach. Intercity coaches serve Porto. The main roads are the A23 and A25.
Climate. Average temperatures are +4.3 °C in January and +20.3 °C in July. July’s average daily high is +26.6 °C (IPMA, 1991–2020).

Education: Universidade Politécnica da Guarda, formerly Instituto Politécnico da Guarda.
Healthcare: Hospital Sousa Martins, ULS da Guarda.

There are 2.32 residents aged 15–64 for each resident aged 65 or over, compared with 2.76 nationwide. Old-age dependency is higher than Portugal’s overall, and four-year population growth was the slowest in the selection. The figures cover the entire municipality.

What investors could buy

Consider a T1 or T2 with efficient heating and a convenient commute to work or classes. COFICAB has sites in Vale de Estrela and at PLIE in Casal de Cinza, so check the distance to the specific site. A cheaper flat farther away may be inconvenient for workers.

What to consider

Check the windows, signs of damp, the roof and heating costs. Population growth was the lowest of the five municipalities. Use comparable local properties to validate the rent.

3. Bragança

Student life in the northeast

Bragança is in Portugal’s far northeast, close to Spain.

Shopping. Continente and Lidl supermarkets.
Transport. Intercity coaches serve Porto and Lisbon. By car, Porto is accessible via the A4. The city has no passenger rail service.
Climate. Average temperatures are +4.8 °C in January and +22.0 °C in July. July’s average daily high is +29.8 °C (IPMA, 1991–2020).

Education: Universidade Politécnica de Bragança, formerly Instituto Politécnico de Bragança.
Healthcare: Unidade Hospitalar de Bragança, ULS do Nordeste.

Bragança has the highest ratio of residents aged 65 or over to children in the selection. It has 2.41 residents aged 15–64 per resident aged 65 or over, more than Guarda, Portalegre and Castelo Branco. The figures cover the entire municipality.

What investors could buy

A T1 or T2 could suit students, lecturers and local workers. A T3 near the relevant campus building may work as a room rental. Flats with a lift and easy access to services may appeal to older residents who find it difficult to live far from town amenities.

What to consider

Population grew fastest here among the five municipalities. A central apartment and a house in a remote village appeal to different buyers, so selling times can vary substantially.

2. Portalegre

A small city with healthcare and manufacturing

Portalegre is in Alto Alentejo, near Spain and the Serra de São Mamede.

Shopping. Continente Modelo on Rua do Joinal and Lidl.
Transport. Intercity coaches serve Lisbon. To reach Porto, you can travel via Lisbon and transfer to a coach or train. Portalegre’s railway station is outside the city.
Climate. Average temperatures are +8.7 °C in January and +24.3 °C in July. July’s average daily high is +31.2 °C (IPMA, 1991–2020).

Education: Universidade Politécnica de Portalegre, formerly Instituto Politécnico de Portalegre.
Healthcare: Hospital Doutor José Maria Grande, ULS do Alto Alentejo.

There are 2.23 residents aged 15–64 per resident aged 65 or over. Old-age dependency is markedly higher than nationwide, where the ratio is 2.76. The figures cover the entire municipality.

What investors could buy

Consider a small central apartment for local workers or a property near the hospital or the relevant teaching building. The university also has facilities in Elvas, so check where students actually attend classes. For a T2 or T3 rented by the room, verify both demand and layout.

What to consider

This is the smallest municipality in the selection. Large houses requiring costly renovations may have a limited pool of future buyers.

1. Castelo Branco

Affordable housing and an industrial base

Castelo Branco is in central inland Portugal, south of Guarda.

Shopping. Alegro Castelo Branco shopping centre and Auchan hypermarket.
Transport. Lisbon is accessible by coach or Intercidades train on the Beira Baixa line. Intercity coaches serve Porto.
Climate. Average temperatures are +8.3 °C in January and +24.9 °C in July. July’s average daily high is +32.5 °C (IPMA, 1991–2020).

Education: Universidade Politécnica de Castelo Branco, formerly Instituto Politécnico de Castelo Branco.
Healthcare: Hospital Amato Lusitano, ULS de Castelo Branco.

There are 2.19 residents aged 15–64 per resident aged 65 or over. This is the lowest ratio of the five, although the municipality’s population grew 7.5% over four years. The figures cover the entire municipality.

On 10 November 2026, the municipality reported that Versigent employed around 1,000 people and approved a lease for its planned expansion. The announcement concerns the company’s plans and gives no indication of how apartment prices will change.

What investors could buy

Consider a practical T2 for a working couple or a family, or a T1/T2 near the hospital and teaching buildings. An apartment with dated finishes and a sound structure may suit a modest renovation and a long-term let. Set the refurbishment budget against local rents.

What to consider

Low purchase prices come with historically lower local earnings. Avoid basing the figures on a single factory or expecting an expensive refurbishment to pay for itself through higher rent.

How much do people earn in these cities?

Average gross monthly earnings across the five municipalities were around €1,200–€1,331. If you are moving for a local job, weigh the actual offer against rent, heating and transport. If you can keep your remote income, lower housing costs may work in your favour. For investors, local earnings help gauge what tenants can afford.

Rents in new and existing homes

The 2025 median for new rental contracts was €4.37–€5.83/m² in the five municipalities, compared with €14.13/m² in Porto and €16.88/m² in Lisbon. For a 60 m² apartment, that is approximately €262–€350 per month, versus €848 and €1,013 respectively.

In 2023, the estimated rent for 60 m² amounted to around 17–23% of one average gross salary in these five municipalities, 42% in Porto and 46% in Lisbon. For your own budget, compare current listings with your take-home income. Advertised rents may exceed the median of signed contracts.

The INE data combine property types, so they provide no separate comparable rental medians for new and existing homes in these five cities. To assess the difference, compare apartments of the same size and layout in the same area, accounting for heating, parking and building condition.

Where both medians were available, new homes sold for 39–93% more than existing homes in 2024.

Prices are rising in affordable cities too

According to Idealista, median asking prices rose by 6.4–20.3% year on year. The final prices agreed at sale can differ from asking prices.

What this means for investors and people looking to move

For investors. Start with who would rent the property and where it would be convenient for them to live. For students, check the relevant campus building, competition from student residences and summer vacancies. For workers, check the actual commute, parking and transport. For families, consider schools, layout and running costs.

Illustrative example: €110,000 for the property, €25,000 for renovation and €10,000 for acquisition costs, furniture and other initial expenses. Total investment: €145,000. At €750 monthly rent and one vacant month, annual receipts would be €8,250. After an assumed €2,250 in running costs and reserves, €6,000 would remain, or around 4.1% before income tax and financing costs. Check each assumption against the actual property.

For people looking to move. Start with Vila Real if you want a university setting and convenient trips to Porto. Look at Castelo Branco and Guarda if you need a train to Lisbon. Consider Bragança if your work or studies are in the northeast. In Portalegre, check how practical your chosen address is without a car. Before buying, compare what is left in your budget after housing and transport and try out your everyday routes.
