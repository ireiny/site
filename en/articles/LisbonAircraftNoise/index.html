Planes overhead: which Lisbon neighbourhoods should homebuyers check for airport noise?

Imagine finding a beautiful flat in Lisbon. A lovely neighbourhood, a metro station, schools and parks nearby, and a pleasant view. Everything looks perfect at the viewing. Then a plane flies directly over the building.

A few minutes later another one follows. And another.

For some people, this is familiar city background noise. For others, it is enough to make everyday life in that home uncomfortable.

Humberto Delgado Airport sits inside Lisbon, surrounded by residential streets, schools, hospitals and universities. Aircraft noise therefore affects more than the homes next to the runway: some neighbourhoods several kilometres away are also exposed.

In February 2025, Mensagem de Lisboa published an investigation into the effects of aircraft noise on the city. It cited an estimate of around 388,000 people in Lisbon and surrounding municipalities exposed to night-time aircraft noise above 45 dB.

Where should buyers pay particular attention, and why is this something to check before making an offer?

1. Which Lisbon neighbourhoods lie beneath flight paths?

Start with the corridors used for arrivals and departures. The south-western approach towards the airport is particularly noticeable, but flights also use the northern side when wind and operating conditions change.

Alvalade and Campo Grande

People love Alvalade for its broad streets, green spaces, schools and shops. Campo Grande offers a large park, universities and excellent connections. Yet parts of both neighbourhoods sit beneath an arrival path.

Flights can be especially noticeable around Jardim do Campo Grande, Cidade Universitária and nearby residential streets. Sometimes the sound interrupts a conversation.

That does not mean every street in Alvalade has the same problem. Conditions can differ dramatically between neighbouring addresses.

Cidade Universitária and Avenidas Novas

Universities, offices, homes and healthcare facilities occupy this part of Lisbon. Aircraft noise can add to the existing sound of busy roads.

Mensagem de Lisboa described how overflying aircraft can interrupt lessons. If you work from home, or have young children, repeated flyovers may matter far more than you expect after a brief viewing.

Campolide and Campo de Ourique

These areas are further from the airport, but some parts are still affected by the approach corridor. The Mensagem de Lisboa article discussed elevated readings on a 2018 noise map in parts of Campolide and Campo de Ourique.

Those readings should not be applied to every flat in either area. In Campolide, road and rail traffic can also be significant, so it is important to consider the combined noise environment.

Alcântara

Aircraft can still be audible nearer the river. Alcântara lies along the south-western approach corridor, with busy roads and rail lines adding other sources of sound.

It is a good example of why checking flight paths alone is not enough.

Olivais and the airport surroundings

Olivais is close to the airport, and parts of it experience aircraft noise and airport-related activity.

The experience at a particular property depends on its exact position, window orientation, insulation and nearby roads. Two flats in the same area can feel very different.

Camarate, Prior Velho, Sacavém and the northern side

When we talk about aircraft noise in Lisbon, we usually think of Alvalade and Campo Grande, where planes regularly pass overhead as they approach the airport for landing. But there is another side of the airport that homebuyers should definitely know about.

These areas include Sacavém, Camarate and Prior Velho, as well as several neighbouring communities in the municipality of Loures. When the runway operates in the northbound direction, aircraft take off towards these residential areas. And, as a general rule, take-offs produce more engine noise than landings.

In Camarate, aircraft can fly at relatively low altitudes. Some residents report that the noise can be so intense that they occasionally have to stop talking until a plane has passed.

The problem also affects communities further north, including Bobadela, São João da Talha and Santa Iria da Azóia.

In 2026, plans were announced to change some northbound departure routes to reduce aircraft noise over certain residential areas. However, it is still too early to guarantee how much quieter any particular address might become.

So, if you're considering buying a home in Sacavém, Camarate or Prior Velho, I strongly recommend checking actual flight paths, reviewing official aircraft noise maps and visiting the property on different days. Ideally, don't rely on just one viewing. Planes may fly in one direction today and another tomorrow.

This is not a list of “bad neighbourhoods”. These are areas where I would make a point of checking aircraft noise before a purchase. Exposure depends on the specific street, weather, time and flight path.

2. Why are aircraft loud one day and barely noticeable the next?

Lisbon Airport has one main runway, which operates in two directions. Aircraft generally take off and land into the wind.

With prevailing northerly winds, many arrivals approach over the south-west of the city. When conditions change, so does the flight pattern.

A flat that seems peaceful during one viewing may be under an active flight path a few days later. One viewing is not enough to assess aircraft noise.

3. Why is aircraft noise more than a nuisance?

People sometimes say, “I’ve got used to the planes.” But getting used to a sound does not mean the body is unaffected by it.

The World Health Organization recommends reducing average aircraft noise below 45 dB Lden and night-time levels below 40 dB Lnight. These are long-term averages, not limits for each individual flyover.

Sleep and recovery

Aircraft can disturb sleep patterns even without fully waking someone. Repeated sleep disturbance is linked to tiredness and difficulties concentrating.

Stress and cardiovascular health

Long-term noise exposure is associated with increased stress and higher risks of certain cardiovascular conditions. These are population-level associations, not a prediction that everyone living near a flight path will become ill.

Children and learning

Research has found associations between aircraft noise and some measures of reading and attention in children. Imagine a classroom where the teacher has to stop talking whenever a plane passes.

4. How loud is it in Lisbon?

Noise maps describe average exposure over time. An individual aircraft can be much louder than that average.

Mensagem de Lisboa highlighted elevated levels in parts of Alvalade and other measurements across the city. But the Lden average and the loudest moment of a flyover are not the same measurement.

For a buyer, both the general background and the frequency of loud events matter, especially at night.

5. Won’t the new airport solve the problem?

Portugal plans to build Luís de Camões Airport at Campo de Tiro de Alcochete to replace Humberto Delgado.

However, government documents do not anticipate its opening before 2034. This is not a guaranteed opening date and schedules may change.

Buying today on the assumption that the planes will disappear within a couple of years would be risky. And moving the airport would not remove other sources of urban noise.

6. How can you check noise before buying a flat?

Start with the official noise maps from Agência Portuguesa do Ambiente (APA). They show areas affected by average day-evening-night and night-time noise. Lisbon’s municipal maps are also helpful because they distinguish aircraft noise from road and other sources.

Next, use ANA Aeroportos’ WebTrak. It shows aircraft tracks, arrivals and departures, altitude and historical movements.

Finally, visit the property at different times: morning, evening and, if possible, at the weekend. Listen with the windows open and closed. Check glazing, ventilation, the bedroom and outdoor spaces such as a balcony or garden.

Good insulation can help indoors, but it will not make your terrace quiet.

7. Aircraft are far from the only source of noise

A property can be far from the airport and still be extremely noisy.

Rail tracks, major roads, late-night venues, restaurant terraces, construction sites, ground-floor businesses and poor sound insulation between flats can all affect day-to-day comfort.

These issues are rarely visible in listing photos, and a twenty-minute viewing may not reveal them.
